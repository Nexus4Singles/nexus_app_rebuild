**It rarely happens all at once.**

**You did not wake up one day as strangers.** It was gradual.

**Nathan described the moment he realized what had been lost.** Nathan said he was sitting across from his wife at dinner, and she was scrolling her phone. "I was scrolling mine. We had not said anything meaningful in twenty minutes. And suddenly I thought--when did we become this? We used to talk for hours. We used to make each other laugh until we cried. Now we just exist in the same space."

**Conversations became logistics.** Date nights became occasional, then rare. Inside jokes faded from memory. You stopped asking about their inner world. Phones replaced eye contact. Responsibilities crowded out connection.

**"Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor: If either of them falls down, one can help the other up. But pity anyone who falls and has no one to help them up" (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10). Marriage was designed for partnership, for companionship. When friendship fades, you lose the very thing that makes the hard work worth it.

**The fade was so slow you did not notice until you looked up and realized: we are not friends anymore.**
